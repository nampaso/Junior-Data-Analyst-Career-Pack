## Business Problem
**Duka Electronics** wants to determine which retail branches and product categories drive revenue and margin growth in H1 2026, diagnose Q2 revenue contractions in Mombasa and Nakuru, and decide where to focus a **KES 15M H2 2026 marketing and promotional budget**.

### Project Brief (Phase 1: Ask)
- **Primary Stakeholder:** Head of Commercial Operations & Marketing, Duka Electronics.
- **Decision Informed:** Reallocation of H2 2026 promotional spend across branches and product lines, plus targeted customer retention interventions.
- **Analytical Questions:**
  1. How did monthly and quarterly revenue, order volume, and gross profit margin trend across branches in H1 2026?
  2. Which product categories generate the highest revenue share versus the highest realized gross profit margin?
  3. Is the Q2 revenue drop in Mombasa and Nakuru driven by lower order frequency (footfall/repeat orders) or smaller basket sizes (Average Order Value)?
  4. Do Gold loyalty customers generate a statistically significantly higher order value than Bronze loyalty customers?
  5. Which branch-category combinations and payment methods deliver the strongest net revenue and lowest return rates?
- **Success Criteria:** Deliver a fully reproducible SQLite + Python analytics pipeline, an interactive Looker Studio dashboard, a filterable Excel stakeholder workbook, and quantified H2 budget recommendations projected to lift H2 gross profit by **12%+**.

---

## Key Findings
- **Nairobi leads total scale; Eldoret leads growth:** Nairobi generates **36%+** of total H1 net revenue, whereas Eldoret recorded the fastest quarter-over-quarter expansion (**~+50% in Q2 vs. Q1**).
- **High-ticket revenue vs. high-margin volume:** Laptops account for **~58%** of net revenue from only **~22%** of completed orders, while Accessories generate high order volume with the strongest gross profit margin (**~42%**).
- **Coastal & Rift Valley Q2 contraction:** Mombasa and Nakuru revenue fell by roughly **a quarter (~23–24%) in Q2**, driven primarily by lower repeat order frequency rather than a drop in Average Order Value.
- **Statistically significant loyalty spend gap:** Gold loyalty members spend significantly more per order than Bronze members (**Mann-Whitney U test, $p < 0.001$**), confirming that targeted loyalty retention outperforms blanket discounting.
- **Mobile money dominance:** M-Pesa accounts for **~58%** of completed transactions across branches, making M-Pesa-linked cashback incentives the lowest-friction retention lever.
